Big Tech Research in Retreat: The Paper Era Is Ending

Research labs at the giants are shutting down, merging, and shrinking. This is not one company’s problem — it is the organizational reconstruction that follows AI industrialization.

Gordon Guocheng Qian
June 20, 2026 · 4 min read
✉ Send to my email

The Research divisions of the tech giants are shutting down, merging, and shrinking, one after another.

The old narrative was: a top technology company must have a top research lab. Research explores the future, publishes at the top venues, attracts the smartest people, and builds the company’s long-term technical moat. Product teams handle deployment; engineering teams handle scale.

That division of labor held up for a long time.

After generative AI, things changed.

Research is no longer something that is “far from product but strategically important.” Research has to feed directly into product, models, data, platforms, and the revenue chain.

So the Research divisions of many giants are being redefined: not an independent kingdom, but part of the business capability; not a paper machine, but the upstream of product iteration; not open-ended long-term exploration, but something measured together with the roadmap, headcount, GPUs, revenue, and user growth.

Some signals

From outside and inside the industry, a series of similar signals:

  • NVIDIA Research: Ming-Yu’s group merged from Research into SWE. Other research groups are essentially not expanding headcount.
  • Meta FAIR: FAIR has been merged into the MSL organization. The independent research brand still exists, but the organizational form is moving ever closer to productized AI capability.
  • Adobe Research: Research cut roughly in half, and merged with Adobe Firefly into a new AI division; headcount is not expanding.
  • Apple MLR: its scope is gradually being absorbed by AI/ML. The boundary between research and platform, product, and systems capability keeps blurring.
  • Snap Research: cut by roughly two-thirds; only the groups highly relevant to the business remain.
  • Netflix Research: the research group has been merged into the business.

Why is this happening now?

One reason is that the center of gravity of AI research has moved.

In the past, a lot of research could build influence through “small but beautiful” breakthroughs: a new loss, a new architecture, a pretty number on a benchmark, a NeurIPS / CVPR / ICML paper — that was enough to prove value.

Today’s frontier AI looks more like heavy industry.

You need a data flywheel, training infrastructure, inference optimization, product feedback, user traffic, evaluation systems, a GPU budget, a model release cadence, and an entire pipeline that can turn a research idea into a production model.

That makes the position of an “independent Research division” awkward. Under scaling laws, genuinely useful research requires massive data, infra, and engineering effort. It is no longer an era where one person can fight alone.

If research is too far from product, the company asks: why are we still paying for this many people?

If research is very close to product, the company asks: then why not merge it directly into the product or engineering organization?

So whichever way it goes, the independent space of the traditional research lab is shrinking.

My takeaway

The wave of shutdowns, mergers, and cuts across big-tech research divisions is not, at its core, a single company’s failure.

It is the organizational reconstruction that follows the industrialization of AI.

When the model itself becomes the core of the product, when training and deployment costs are high enough to demand serious return on investment, and when a paper advantage no longer automatically converts into a market advantage, Research has to prove itself all over again.

It is not that the giants no longer need Research. The giants are no longer willing to pay for things that look like Research but never enter the system.

The paper era has not ended.

But the era in which the value of Research is defined by papers alone is ending.

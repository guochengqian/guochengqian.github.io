This version replaces the old bottom-up sheet. Instead of combining Nvidia revenue shares, campus power and press targets into my own ranges, it uses Epoch AI’s 2026 AI Chip Owners raw database. Hyperscalers use the latest complete common cutoff, 2025-12-31, while xAI is updated separately to Epoch’s July 2026 site estimate. The figures are useful for scale, not audited asset counts.

“Ownership” here is closer to control than accounting title. Epoch explicitly attributes cloud compute to the cloud provider, not the AI lab renting it. That removes OpenAI as a standalone row: most of its capacity sits under providers such as Microsoft, Oracle and CoreWeave. It also makes Oracle and CoreWeave necessary standalone rows. Joint ventures, project finance and customer-supplied hardware can still complicate legal ownership.

H100e is not a physical card count. Epoch converts dense 8-bit peak throughput; for example, one TPUv7 is about 2.3 H100e. The right column only asks how much facility power a physical-H100 fleet would need to provide the same peak performance; it is not Google’s, Amazon’s or xAI’s actual electricity use.

xAI makes the distinction concrete. Epoch’s July 2026 estimate puts Colossus 1 + 2 at roughly 1.39M H100e / 1.29GW of IT power. Replacing that mixed fleet with physical H100s would imply about 2.3GW of facility load. The difference reflects Blackwell and other higher-performance chips inside the H100e total. The reported nearly 2GW is the planned supply target after the third building is completed.

Appendix · Where this sheet is shaky

Where this sheet is shaky

Has a hard source

Nvidia’s data-center revenue by fiscal year (10-K / 8-K); the $500B and $1T order figures (company statements); Omdia’s 2022/2023 unit counts; the big-four clouds’ 133GW power deals; the on-line months and planned power of the five 1GW+ sites; Meta’s disclosed H100 clusters; Project Rainier’s nearly 500k Trainium2 (AWS official); CoreWeave’s 850MW active at end-2025 and more than 1GW in Q1 2026 (company results).

Estimate or my own derivation

The compute-owner table’s H100e ranges are Epoch AI model estimates, not audited company disclosures; the right column is merely an H100-equivalent facility-load conversion at 600k H100e/GW, not each owner’s actual power draw; Oracle has the sparsest evidence and widest interval; each site’s “live” power (mostly reported estimates outside a few disclosures); the 2026 revenue estimate; and the card range back-solved from order value.

Nobody published it

Nvidia’s data-center GPU units shipped for 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2024. Any chart claiming these numbers most likely back-solved them from revenue divided by an assumed ASP — including me, if I did that.

Meta Has Everything — Except a Culture

A one-year review of Meta Superintelligence: data, compute, talent, and half the planet’s users. Meta has it all — except the one thing hardest to buy: culture.

Gordon Guocheng Qian
July 10, 2026 · 3 min read

SemiAnalysis published a one-year review of Meta Superintelligence that lays out every card in Meta’s hand:

✅ Data
Around 3,000 engineers working full-time to build RL tasks and environments — 70% of them this year’s new grads.

✅ Compute
Five “titan” clusters above 1GW under construction at once — Prometheus in Ohio, Hyperion in Louisiana, plus three more in El Paso, Iowa, and Indiana.
How big is 1GW? In 2024, not a single datacenter on Earth ran at 1GW. In 2026, exactly five cross that line worldwide: Amazon, xAI, Microsoft, Meta, and OpenAI — one each.
In other words: five in the whole world, and Meta alone is building five. Meta expects to deploy about 6.5GW of AI compute by the end of 2026, with 5.5GW of that landing in the second half of the year.

✅ Talent
Nine-figure annual pay, and sometimes offers north of a billion dollars.

With talent and compute at that level, catching up on the model is only a matter of time.

And the best card of all: half the planet’s population are Meta’s users. Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook reach half the world, across every age group and income bracket. A user base, a data stream, and a distribution channel of that scale simply can’t be bought.

But it’s missing the one thing hardest to replace: culture.

Meta is the famous “squid-game factory.” The prize is big enough that people come — but everyone knows they came for the money, and everyone knows the person next to them could be cut at any moment.

The day Meta gets back the culture it lost, it truly takes off — wings and all. I just went all in on Meta stock. Placing the bet.

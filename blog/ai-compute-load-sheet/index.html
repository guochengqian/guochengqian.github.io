Part 3 · Player overview

Cards and power, company by company

Player GPUsH100-equiv · 10k Online powerGW · plugged in now Stated goal Notes

Google and Amazon’s compute mostly doesn’t come from Nvidia GPUs — it’s their own TPUs and Trainium, which are technically ASICs, not GPUs, folded into H100-equivalents here. So “who has the most cards” and “who pays Nvidia the most” are not the same question.

How the GPU ranges are derived (all folded into H100-equivalents). Google: Epoch AI (Oct 2024) estimates >1M equivalents; 2026 TPU shipments are forecast at ~4.3M (incl. external sales), folded at 1.0–1.5 equivalents each → 2.5–4.5M. Meta: Zuckerberg (Jan 2025) says over 1.3M physical cards by end-2025, plus H1-2026 Prometheus at 1GW (~500–600k GB200-class per GW) → 1.8–2.5M cards, ×1.2 for mixed generations → 2.2–3.0M equivalents. Microsoft: Nvidia’s 2023–2025 cumulative data-center revenue ~$356B, Microsoft long ~15% of it (the 10-Q “Customer A”) → ~$53B, at $30k–40k system-level ASP → 1.3–1.8M physical cards, Blackwell folded at 2–2.5× → 2.0–3.0M equivalents. Amazon: ~1.4M Trainium across three generations (0.7–0.9 equiv/chip) plus the Nvidia fleet (~6% of Nvidia revenue) → 1.8–2.6M equivalents. xAI: 555k physical cards, H100/H200/GB200 mixed → 1.0–1.3M equivalents. OpenAI: very little self-owned, back-solved from Abilene’s 0.3GW live → 0.2–0.4M equivalents.

How the online-power figures are derived. Except xAI (2GW is reported) and OpenAI (Abilene runs 0.3GW live), the company-level AI power for the other four is undisclosed by anyone. I back-solve it from card counts using “~600k H100-equivalents ≈ 1 GW,” then reconcile with each company’s disclosed build rate (Microsoft ~2GW built in 2025 plus ~1GW each in FY26 Q2/Q3; Amazon +3.8GW in 2025; Meta’s Prometheus past 3GW+). So the cards and power columns aren’t independent evidence — they’re cross-calibrated. Their agreement only shows internal consistency, not that the absolute numbers are right.

Three reasons these numbers are unreliable, said plainly. One: Nvidia’s 10-Q “direct customer” is often an ODM/contract manufacturer, not the end cloud, so identifying Customer A as Microsoft is itself an inference; and one-fifth-ish of data-center revenue is networking gear, not GPUs. Two: “H100-equivalent” is itself an unstable unit — the same GB300 counts three-plus times as much under FP4 inference as under FP16 training. Cross-company comparisons are far shakier than they look. Three: every company measures differently, and none of it is audited. So the true error on these ranges could reach a factor of two. Read the magnitude, not the digits.

Appendix · Where this sheet is shaky

Where this sheet is shaky

Has a hard source

Nvidia’s data-center revenue by fiscal year (10-K / 8-K); the $500B and $1T order figures (company statements); Omdia’s 2022/2023 unit counts; the big-four clouds’ 133GW power deals; the on-line months and planned power of the five 1GW+ sites; Meta’s 1.3M GPUs by end-2025 (Zuckerberg, Jan 2025); Project Rainier’s ~500k Trainium2 live and scaling to 1M (AWS official).

Estimate or my own derivation

The GPU and online-power columns of the overview table — all six are my estimates by the methods above, possibly off by a factor of two, and the two columns are cross-calibrated rather than independent; each site’s “live” power (mostly reported estimates outside a few disclosures); the 2026 revenue estimate; the card range back-solved from the order value; xAI’s 2GW / 555k cards are a January 2026 report; Trainium’s cumulative 1.4M is a reported figure.

Nobody published it

Nvidia’s data-center GPU units shipped for 2019, 2020, 2021 and 2024. Any chart claiming these numbers most likely back-solved them from revenue divided by an assumed ASP — including me, if I did that.

Priced In Is Not the Same as Exhausted

META’s move today reminded me why “it’s already priced in” is often too lazy. Expectation, confirmation, and scale are different stages.

Gordon Guocheng Qian
July 1, 2026 · 5 min read

Today META was up roughly 10%, simply because the market started to take its cloud / AI infrastructure story more seriously.

The funny thing is that this was not a new idea to us.

One month ago, when we discussed META, the thesis was already obvious: Meta is one of the strongest cloud-player candidates outside the usual hyperscalers, and maybe the strongest content-distribution machine on earth. Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp. Distribution, users, data, AI talent, capex, internal infra, recommendation systems. If you were trying to imagine a company that could turn AI infra into both internal productivity and external cloud products, Meta was always on the shortlist.

So my first reaction was: wasn’t this already priced in?

Today reminded me that “priced in” is one of the most abused phrases in the market.

There are at least two different things people mix together.

First, there is the information gap. Some people have better information, better understanding, or simply better vision. They can see the structure earlier: who owns distribution, who owns infra, who can turn internal capability into external products, who benefits when the bottleneck moves from model training to inference and agents.

Second, there is whether the bullish or bearish news has actually been exhausted. In the short run, that often matters more than the abstract question of whether smart people “already knew” the thesis.

A stock can have a thesis that is “priced in” at the expectation level, but still move up when the event lands.

Why? Because expectation is not the same as confirmation.

When the news becomes real, the market does not just say: “OK, we knew that.” It often moves to the next question.

Can this scale? Can this become revenue? Can this change margins? Can this become a new narrative that more funds are forced to own?

That is how a stock goes from “maybe” to “real,” and then from “real” to “bigger than we thought.”

Tesla is a clean example. People can say robotaxi is already priced in. They can say robotics is already priced in. Maybe it is, partially. But if robotaxi actually lands, works, expands city by city, and becomes a visible revenue line, the stock does not have to stop because some investors expected it before. Meeting the expectation can create the next expectation.

The market often pays twice:

  1. once for the dream,
  2. again for the proof.

Sometimes it pays a third time for scale.

This is also why I still think the memory / CPU / AI-infra trade has another leg.

People already say agent scaling is priced in. HBM is priced in. DRAM is priced in. AI servers are priced in. AMD, Intel, Micron, SK Hynix, Marvell. All known. All obvious.

But obvious to whom?

Right now, most people still understand agents as coding assistants or chatbots with tools. That is not the end state.

The real next step is scalable agent workflows: OpenAI, Claude, and others releasing loops where hundreds or thousands of agents can work simultaneously, each with memory, context, tools, verification, and coordination. Not just for programmers. For everyday users, teams, companies, research, finance, operations, shopping, planning, customer service, design, data analysis.

A single chatbot is one stream of tokens.

A 1,000-agent workflow is a small organization made of tokens.

That changes the infrastructure math.

More agents means more inference. More inference means more memory bandwidth, more memory capacity, more CPU orchestration, more networking, more storage, more verification loops, more retries, more context movement. The bottleneck does not disappear because “AI infra was already priced in.” It becomes visible in a larger way when the product form changes.

This is why I can still imagine another major repricing in Micron, SK Hynix, Intel, AMD, and Marvell if agent scaling becomes a daily-user product rather than only a developer product.

Not because the market has never heard of AI memory.

It has.

But because the market may still be underestimating the step function from chatbot usage to parallel agent labor.

My takeaway from META today:

“Priced in” is not enough. Ask which stage the thesis is in.

Rumor Announcement Product Scale Revenue Earnings revision New baseline expectation

A stock can look expensive at the rumor stage and still be cheap if the product reaches scale. A stock can look fully priced on the first-order thesis and still move if the second-order thesis gets stronger.

So I am trying to be more careful with one lazy sentence:

“It is already priced in.”

Maybe.

But is the good news exhausted?

Has the market moved from expectation to confirmation?

And after confirmation, what is the next expectation?

That is where the real move often starts.
